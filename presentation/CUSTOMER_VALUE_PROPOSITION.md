# PACT — Customer Value Proposition

---

## Target Customer Profile

**Who**: A 25-year-old graduate student or early-career professional in an urban area. They earn $50–80K, have discretionary spending habits they recognize as wasteful, and have a financial goal (travel, student loans, emergency fund) that feels slow to reach.

**Behavioral profile**: They order food delivery 3–5 times per week ($15–25 per order). They have 2–4 close friends with similar habits. They've tried budgeting apps but stopped using them within weeks. They've talked with friends about "cutting back on DoorDash" but no one follows through.

---

## Problem Anatomy

**What**: Impulsive convenience spending erodes savings potential. The customer knows this but fails to change because the decision happens at the point of craving, not the point of planning.

**Root causes**:
1. **Intention-action gap**: Plans to save are made in a rational state; spending decisions happen in a convenience-seeking state
2. **No immediate consequence**: Skipping a delivery order saves $20 but feels like nothing in the moment
3. **No accountability**: Solo decisions are easy to rationalize ("I deserve it," "just this once")
4. **Invisible progress**: Savings from not spending are harder to see than savings deposits

**Scale**: Americans aged 25–34 spend an average of $300+/month on food delivery (2024 data). The potential savings from reducing this by even 50% is $1,800+/year.

## PACT's Value Proposition

### Functional Value
- Structures an informal group commitment into a trackable challenge
- Connects daily behavior to savings progress (each successful day = $X toward goal)
- Provides clear outcome rules (15 product rules governing results)

### Emotional Value
- Pride from streaks and successful days
- Sense of control over spending
- Fun from consequence mechanism (dare culture, not punishment)
- Progress visibility toward a meaningful goal

### Social Value
- Shared experience with friends (not saving alone)
- Playful competition and bragging rights
- Consequence content is shareable/viral
- Circle identity and belonging

### Friction PACT Introduces
- Download and setup time (~3 minutes)
- Daily check-in effort (~10 seconds)
- Bank connection (privacy concern, even when simulated)
- Social pressure (some users may find this stressful)
- Consequence risk (may deter risk-averse users)

## Net Benefit Analysis

**Incremental benefits over group chat**:
- Sustained motivation through gamification (streak rings, XP, celebrations)
- Structured accountability (not just goodwill)
- Savings progress visualization
- Fun consequence mechanism
- Repeat engagement through renewal

**Incremental costs over group chat**:
- App download and setup
- Daily check-in habit (small but nonzero)
- Privacy — app knows more than a group chat
- Social pressure may feel higher with visible scores
- Consequence mechanism may cause friction in some friendships

**What must be true for net benefit to be positive**:
1. The gamification (streaks, XP, celebrations) must sustain engagement beyond what enthusiasm provides
2. The consequence mechanism must feel fun, not punitive — participation must be genuinely voluntary
3. The savings visualization must create enough "progress pride" to justify daily check-in effort
4. At least one person in a friend group must be willing to set up the pact and invite others
5. The privacy trade-off (bank connection) must feel safe enough to proceed

---

## Adoption and Rejection Analysis

### Why customers might adopt PACT
1. They've already tried informal group commitments → PACT is a better version of what they want
2. The consequence mechanism is genuinely fun → social content, not punishment
3. Seeing savings grow from daily success is more motivating than tracking spending
4. One enthusiastic friend can recruit the rest
5. Low stakes (no money at risk) reduces adoption friction

### Why customers might reject PACT
1. "I don't want another app tracking my spending" → privacy concern
2. "Our group chat works fine" → insufficient perceived improvement
3. "The consequence feels awkward" → social discomfort threshold varies
4. "I don't have a consistent friend group for this" → Circle dependency
5. "I'll just download an app blocker" → simpler solution preference
6. "Checking in every day is a chore" → habit formation is hard
