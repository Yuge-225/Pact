# PACT — Portfolio Notes

## Key PM Decisions and Reasoning

### 1. Days, Not Dollars
**Decision**: Measure challenge performance by successful eligible days, not money saved.

**Why**: Dollar-based leaderboards create income comparison and shame. A student saving $5/day and a professional saving $20/day both succeed equally when they both avoid delivery apps on eligible days. This makes the product inclusive and focuses on behavior change, which is the actual value.

### 2. No Financial Penalties
**Decision**: Consequences are social and playful — never financial.

**Why**: Financial penalties create a business model that profits from user failure. They also create anxiety that undermines the fun, social experience we're building. Karaoke Confession > losing $20 to a pool. The consequence should motivate through social bonding, not financial fear.

### 3. One Mode Only
**Decision**: No "Competition Mode" vs. "Together Mode" toggle.

**Why**: Mode selection adds cognitive load to an already multi-step creation flow. The single mode IS both competitive (leaderboard, consequences) and collaborative (same rules, group celebration when everyone succeeds). Reducing configuration surface area makes the product easier to explain and adopt.

### 4. Group Celebration Over Manufactured Losers
**Decision**: When everyone achieves perfect completion, celebrate the group — don't create a false hierarchy.

**Why**: Manufacturing a loser when everyone succeeded would undermine trust in the fairness of the system. The celebration when everyone wins is a powerful positive reinforcement that makes the next Pact more likely.

### 5. At-the-Moment Intervention
**Decision**: Show a friction screen when the user tries to open a temptation app, not just track spending after the fact.

**Why**: The core insight is that savings goals fade at the moment of a spending decision. Post-hoc tracking (which is what budgeting apps do) explains yesterday but doesn't change the next decision. Pact needs to be present AT the decision point. This is the key differentiation from every budgeting app.

### 6. Food Delivery as Initial Wedge
**Decision**: Start with food delivery spending, not general budgeting.

**Why**: Food delivery is the #1 discretionary spending category for our target demo (24-35 year olds). It's frequent (multiple times per week), emotionally driven (convenience, stress), and socially visible (friends know you order too much). It's also a category where the alternative (cooking) has positive secondary effects.

### 7. Pre-Agreed Consequences
**Decision**: The consequence is chosen BEFORE the challenge starts and everyone must accept it.

**Why**: Post-hoc consequence selection creates conflict and feels punitive. Pre-agreed consequences feel fair because everyone knew the stakes. The consent moment also creates social buy-in — accepting the Karaoke Confession is itself a commitment device.

### 8. Gentle Unresolved State
**Decision**: If someone doesn't complete their consequence, the app offers reminders, substitutes, and waivers — not escalation.

**Why**: Escalating punishment for a fun social dare would poison the Circle dynamic. The unresolved state should feel socially meaningful ("hey, you still owe us a song") but not hostile. The social pressure of the Circle seeing "Unresolved" is itself the consequence.

## Frameworks Applied

### CIRCLES (Problem Definition)
- **C**omprehend: Young professionals struggle with discretionary spending despite wanting to save
- **I**dentify: The user is someone with savings intent but execution gaps
- **R**eport needs: Accountability at the moment of temptation, not after the fact
- **C**ut through prioritization: Focus on one behavior (delivery) before expanding
- **L**ist solutions: Social challenge + savings allocation + playful consequences
- **E**valuate tradeoffs: Social fun vs. financial seriousness — chose fun
- **S**ummarize: Pact turns saving from a solo willpower exercise into a shared social game

### Jobs-to-be-Done
- **Functional**: Avoid unnecessary spending on committed days
- **Emotional**: Feel proud of restraint instead of deprived
- **Social**: Prove follow-through to friends and hold them to the same standard

### Customer Value Proposition vs. Next-Best Alternative
The next-best alternative is a group chat + bank auto-transfer + willpower. Pact wins on: structured commitment, visible progress, at-the-moment intervention, integrated savings tracking, and fun social consequences.

## Metrics I Would Track (Post-Prototype)
1. **Pact completion rate**: % of Pacts where at least one member achieves perfect score
2. **Renewal rate**: % of completed Pacts that lead to a new Pact within 7 days
3. **Savings allocation rate**: % of eligible days that result in a savings allocation
4. **Circle retention**: % of Circles that complete 3+ Pacts together
5. **Temptation shield effectiveness**: % of app interceptions where user chooses "Keep My Pact"
6. **Consequence completion rate**: % of assigned consequences that reach "Verified" status
